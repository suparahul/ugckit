## What the evidence says together

1. **Reach comes from the format, not the follower count.** `@mei.dates` has 1,191 followers and posts above 500K views. `@lindalewiston3` has 376 followers and a 149.6K-view post. `@mognutrition` has 90 followers and a 422.4K-view post. The account is a distribution channel. The format carries the reach.
2. **The app stays out of the slides and lives in the bio.** `@mei.dates` sells with one bio word, "coffeemeetsbagel girlie". `@lindalewiston3` has the two-word bio "Relationship coach". `@testo.cat` sends viewers to a linktree. Two exceptions put the product on a late slide: cray cray and Locket. One exception puts it on slide two: the "wait… let me grab my camera" format. Lino Leighton puts a first-person CTA slide at slide 4 or 5, after three value slides.
3. **One slot template repeats, with the noun or the verb rotated.** `@testo.cat` runs "5 [things / signs / habits] + verb + testosterone". `@male_aestheticcc` runs "Every man needs 4 X". `@healthyyinspoo` runs "Healthy [meal] ideas". cray cray runs "5 uncomfortable questions to ask your other half". The shape is count + category noun + discomfort word, in three independent niches.
4. **Faces are optional.** A mascot works (`@testo.cat`). A stock photo works (`@lindalewiston3`, cray cray). A statue works (`@captaingreek_`). A cartoon works (`@vdrophealth`). A persona with no face works (`@mei.dates`). Social Growth Engineers state it: "Faceless content is no longer a niche format."
5. **Many accounts run one format, and non-English accounts go first.** cray cray runs 25+ faceless accounts. Vent Now runs dozens. Salim says "one proven format, many accounts, posting daily. that's the engine." Lucas Patiri says "french, spanish, portuguese accounts consistently outperform english-only". cray cray's best account is French.
6. **Saves, not watch time, are the metric a slideshow sells.** Vent Now's post did 99K bookmarks on 1.8M views. cray cray's post did 85K saves on 2.8M views.
7. **Measure past the view.** The GLP-1 campaign says "A post that gets fewer views but brings paying users can be worth a lot more to you." Melv says "slideshows dont have to be viral to convert, just do the right research". Thrive's views are proven. Its revenue is not: "That app isn't making money tho lol."

## Account Architecture

**How many accounts.**

- Run a network, not one account. Salim: "one proven format, many accounts, posting daily. that's the engine." Patiri's faceless-network playbook: 5–25 accounts. cray cray runs 25+. Vent Now runs dozens. Wider claims. @iamgdsa: 7 accounts, $50K MRR, 10M views. @gauravsbuilding: one account at 50M+ views from one repeated format. @Jarthurwins: 3M+ views and 10K MRR from own accounts.
- Accounts per device. Pedro: "You can have up to 8 TikTok accounts on one device." He runs 6-7. Salim: "up to 10 accounts per device before tiktok starts flagging you." Disagreement, untested by us.
- Set-up rules from Pedro. Create accounts with an email, not a phone number. Wait 24-48 hours between accounts. Create the account 2-3 days before the first post and scroll 15-20 minutes before you post. Kill rule: "consistently getting 0 views across 7-8 posts".
- Foreign market. Pedro: factory-reset phone, no SIM, US location, English, NordVPN to New York, kept on. Salim borrows native accounts through Discord and rejects VPNs: "too fragile, too much babysitting." Disagreement, untested by us.
- Exception 1: an SEO account needs no network. Nicholas's result came from one account, about 80 posts over 3 months. To scale SEO, add accounts in different niches: "Create several accounts, different niches, same system."
- Exception 2: one large account. `@glowuptrickz` has 0 following, 1.6M followers and the App Store link in the bio.
- The cost floor for volume. Ethan Nguyen quotes one operator at "roughly $22/month to run 12 AI slideshow personas posting daily. That's 360 slideshow attempts a month." His point: "the economics have to allow volume." His arithmetic: one account at one post a day is about 30 attempts a month. 10 accounts at two a day is about 600.
- Brain's cadence: "2 posts/day 10 accounts 20 shots at distribution every single day". Setup is "4-6 hours on this for 3 days" for "2+ months of content". Most posts flop below 300 views. A few do 50K, 200K, 500K, sometimes 1M+. One of his hit 5M.
- Laur's volume model: "the person posting 1000 times a month has a 33x higher chance of catching a massive viral outlier than the person posting 30 times". No source for the 33x. His pipeline has four steps. Scrape the 40 best slideshows in the niche. Map hook, build and payoff with an LLM. Generate 1200+ variations. Post through aged accounts. The bottleneck named in the replies is warmed accounts.
- Accounts are an asset. The AI garden design app was listed for sale with its TikTok accounts included.

**How to name them.**

- Distribution accounts read as people or theme pages, not brands. No handle carries the app name. Lino: "Create a separate TikTok account just for organic content." It must feel like "a real niche content page". "The app comes later ( THIS IS KEY)". His example, `@thatgymguy16`, has the bio "Collecting gym rats 0/1000. Page dedicated to the gym." Vent Now uses first-name personas: "sydney" `@sydneysynced`, "sarah" `@selfbysarah`, "heather <3" `@heather.xoxo`.
- Four persona types work. A person (`@mei.dates`, one invented woman in New York). A mascot (`@testo.cat`, a ginger cat). A pure distribution page (`@glowuptrickz`, 0 following). A recurring AI character (Pedro's crying woman, the same face in every post, 7.1M views in one week, bio "The app I use… on App Store").
- The bio is short and holds the only pointer to the app. `@mei.dates`: "coffeemeetsbagel girlie", no link. `@lindalewiston3`: "Relationship coach", no link. `@testo.cat`: "your T is cooked. testo cat is here to fix it" plus a linktree. Vent Now personas: soft self-help lines like "self-improvement".
- Follower count is not the goal. Vent Now accounts sit at 14-21K followers with posts at 5.8M, 7.5M, 4.8M and 2.6M views.

**How to distribute formats.**

- One locked format per account. Every account in the set runs one template and rotates the noun or the verb.
- One template on several persona accounts. Groundly runs at least two, `@lindalewiston3` (Linda) and `@lin444782` (Lin). The same cover titles repeat across both accounts and inside each account, with different couple photos. "feminine ways to talk to him" did 366.9K on Lin against 61.2K on Linda. The couple photos look AI-generated, with the same two faces on many covers.
- Same format on many accounts, different packaging. Patiri: "Same content, different packaging" (colours, layout, hook style). Salim: a format that hits on one account hits on the others, and the repeated format becomes the brand.
- Localize. "french, spanish, portuguese accounts consistently outperform english-only". cray cray's best account is French, `@questionsprofondes1`, 6M views a month for over a year.
- Cadence. Lino: 3–4 posts a day, "This is a volume game." New accounts warm up for a few weeks at 2–4 posts a day. Patiri: 1–3 posts per day per account. Pedro, when testing: "3 different formats per account, per day." Nicholas, for SEO: "at least 1 time per day for at least 3 months".
- Warm the research account too. Nicholas: TikTok suggests keywords only to an active account.
- Own the niche (claim, no data). Yassr: "the second they do, they get served another one of your posts / you're everywhere, and your app is always sitting in one of the slides / so a normal user thinks "this app has genuinely helped so many people" / not "this is an ad"". On `@oliviatok88`, the one account of the four opened, the app is named as plain text inside one body slide ("wellness ai"), `ACCOUNT-INSPECT-oliviatok88-2026-09-24.md` § 3.

## Format Portfolio

- Slideshow against video, three verdicts. Pedro: "slideshows generally convert worse than videos. However, they're MUCH faster to produce." Salim: "they convert just as well as video, and they move an absurd amount of views compared to videos." Nicholas: "slideshows can be done with AI from start to finish." Pedro's advice: "Don't choose one. Test both."
- A slideshow is "basically locked to TikTok". A video repurposes to Reels, Shorts and Facebook. Matt Welter on product type: "ios apps are (by far) the best for slideshows, 2nd would be ecom/tts".
- Formats transfer across niches. Pedro's six transferable formats: phone notification slideshows, single-image slideshows with long text, UGC reactions, screen recordings, before/after, storytelling. Salim: take the concept even when the subject differs.
- Speed decides the first formats. Pedro: "Prioritize formats that take less than 10–15 minutes to replicate." Collect 12-15 formats before you start.
- Who the faceless network fits, per Patiri: apps where the content is the product output (questions, stickers, results, scores). Who it does not fit: "Apps that need trust: finance, health, education." Note the conflict: `@testo.cat`, `@glowuptrickz` and `@mognutrition` are health accounts that win on slideshows.
- Production tooling is disputed. Salim: "there is no tool that produces slideshows at human-level quality yet… so the actual slides get made by hand." Nicholas: Canva plus AI images (Midjourney, Nano Banana, GPT-5), split in PineTools, text added inside TikTok, now scalable "x100". The GLP-1 campaign gave creators a Gemini tutorial.
- Images come from Pinterest. Search the niche plus "aesthetic": "gym aesthetic", "looksmaxxing aesthetic", "baseball aesthetic". All slides share one visual style, full-screen, never letterboxed. Lino: "Five random images feel thrown together. Five images with the same aesthetic feel like an actual content page." This matches cray cray's "couple photo from pinterest in muted colors".
- Pinterest beats polished AI images. Pedro: "around 80% of the images I use for app marketing come from Pinterest… these kinds of images often perform better than overly polished AI-generated visuals." Prefer images without visible faces. Brain builds a Pinterest reference library per slide type, then generates variations from it with an image API.
- The "Apps I use…" list post is a top performer. On a hybrid-athlete grid where every other post is under 60K, "Apps I use as a hybrid athlete" did 672.7K and "Apps I use as a gym/run enthusiast" did 174.7K.
- A format switch moves an account. Salim's screen recording: `@the.modern.man` runs one editorial template (black headline, key noun in red, black-and-white portrait) with a top post of 457.6K. `@gym1495` copied the template into the gym niche. Its older posts sit at 363–6,481 views. Its six newest posts sit at 17.3K–113.5K. Salim's claim, "jumped from 4k views on average to over 170k", is larger than the frames show.
- Sound is a ranked factor. Pedro: the music must match the emotion, never a trending sound that contradicts the story. Lino: match the aesthetic and the emotion, and copy what winning slideshow accounts in the niche use now. "The Sound has more of a impact than you think." No other source ranks sound.
- The hook line travels across mediums. On "Kind of chic...": "Video or slideshow, you decide." Pick the hook first and the medium second.
- Slideshows are the fallback when paid stops. @getnoise: "slideshows were the only thing keeping my app alive at $1500 MRR when I got banned from TikTok ads".
- A complete slideshow (Tjay): name the problem → explain the mechanism → show an example → handle an objection → offer an implementation asset. "The asset compresses execution. It does not ransom the answer." The weak structure: name a problem, tease a secret, withhold the method, demand a comment.
- Build CTA variants on one approved base deck: A direct product action, B saveable resource, C optional conversation. Review the whole variant, because a different close can need a different setup earlier.
- Virality is not required. Melv: "slideshows dont have to be viral to convert, just do the right research". $500 MRR in five days with 0 audience.

## Format Specs

Compact specs. Only known fields are listed. The unknowns are collected at the end of the section.

**Keyword-targeted SEO slideshow** — Nicholas Dulait. Niche: cooking, and his unnamed B2C app.
- Structure: 7 slides. The keyword sits in four places. Slide 1 text, typed with TikTok's text tool. The title. The description. 3-5 hashtags led by #creatorsearchinsights. Example: "Best struggle meals" on `@easy_cooking_ideas`.
- Hook: the keyword is the hook.
- Engagement: one detail wrong on purpose. "I purposely added some slightly expensive ingredients to provoke people and it hit the spot".
- Visual: Canva template plus AI images.
- Evidence: 3.8M views over twelve months from one account. Traffic source: Search 99.3%, For You 0%. Still 10K views a day twelve months after the last post.

**Mascot listicle** — `@testo.cat` for Thrive. Niche: testosterone.
- Structure: cover = cat photo + numbered title + "(explained by testo cat)".
- Hook: "5 [things / signs / habits] + verb + testosterone". Verbs rotate RAISING, BOOSTING, KILLING.
- Product: bio link only. Whether the slides name the app is unknown.
- Visual: one ginger cat on every cover. No face.
- Evidence: 437.2K, 403.3K, 243.2K on 8,699 followers.

**Persona story** — `@mei.dates` for Coffee Meets Bagel. Niche: NYC dating.
- Structure: "1. bold question hook about dating / 2. relatable NYC dating anecdotes / 3. soft sells CMB as the better dating app".
- Hook: a question or a confession, never a claim.
- Product: never in the content. One bio word, "coffeemeetsbagel girlie".
- Visual: a text card over a moody photo. No product.
- Evidence: 582.1K, 518.9K, 512K on 1,191 followers.

**Stock-photo relationship listicle** — `@lindalewiston3` for Groundly. Niche: relationships.
- Structure: "1. bold hook slide like "5 uncomfortable laws..." / 2. numbered relationship tips / 3. subtle soft selling ios app".
- Hook: count + relationship promise, small text top left. Attachment words (avoidant, anxious) do the targeting.
- Visual: soft-focus outdoor couple photo (lake, dune, beach, sunset). No face, no product.
- Repetition: "feminine ways to talk to him" posted three times with different photos, 61.2K, 39.8K, 24.1K.
- Evidence: 149.6K, 139.2K on 376 followers.

**Pinterest couple photo + in-app screenshots** — cray cray. Niche: couples game.
- Structure: "couple photo from pinterest in muted colors + text hook overlay ('5 uncomfortable questions to ask your other half'). followed by in-app screenshots with questions and the app logo visible."
- Product: late slides, as the payoff. This is the closest match to the format this repo studies.
- Metric: saves. One post did 2.8M views and 85K saves.
- Evidence: 25+ accounts, 130M views in 14 months. Named by two independent sources.

**Numbered psychology list on a night-sky photo** — Vent Now. Niche: mental health.
- Structure: one numbered item per slide, heading then body. Seen: "2) backwards journaling" / "write your day's events in reverse order to rewire how your brain processes memory and time".
- Hook: count or category + hidden psychological cause.
- Visual: a real starry night-sky photo, pale yellow centred text. Faceless.
- Metric: 8.1M views / 96K bookmarks and 1.8M views / 99K bookmarks.

**Soft-life self-improvement** — `@sydneysynced` (Vent Now). Visual: aesthetic photos of one young woman, plain white sans-serif text. Hooks: "5 viral theories that will ACTUALLY change your life" 4.8M, "how to become more confident" 1.6M. Note "4 apps that will make you disgustingly productive", a hook with a slot for a product.

**Relationship advice on couples photography** — `@selfbysarah`, `@heather.xoxo` (Vent Now). Hooks: "what men don't understand about women (but REALLY should)" 7.9M, "how to love someone who gets anxious in relationships" 7.5M. Device: numbered series, "a guide on how to be a better girlfriend (part 5)" 2.6M.

**"Every man needs 4 X" listicle** — `@male_aestheticcc`. Visual: a moody male photo under the number-four promise, then four item slides. Evidence: "Every man needs 4 discipline habits" 739.8K, "Every man needs 4 income skills" 442.2K. The account already links an app from the bio.

**"X ideas" category card** — `@healthyyinspoo`. Structure: slide 1 is a plain white card with the category in a serif font and sparkle marks. Then food photos. Hooks: "Healthy snack ideas" 7.6M, "Healthy dinner ideas" 3M. The same three words recycle. Views spread two orders of magnitude. No product behind it.

**Gym slideshow, three art directions on one niche.** `@gymmadesimple`: white background, grey anatomy figure with the worked muscle in orange, "SAVE THIS FOR LATER" badge burned into every slide. `@vdrophealth`: flat cartoon bodybuilders in red/teal/purple, condensed headline; "SIX-DAY GYM SPLIT" 1.7M. `@captaingreek_`: cream background, marble statue with the target muscle painted red, serif titles; "PULL DAY" 1.7M and 1.6M. The art direction is the differentiator, not the topic.

**Overheard-quote gym slideshow** — `@strongermobile` (Stronger). Structure: a quote in quotation marks over a phone-shot gym photo, POV or mirror. A clown emoji marks a hater's line. Evidence: "remember it's okay to skip the gym today" 2.7M and 3801 on two posts, "I've benched 100kg" 2M. The line does not set the number.

**Medical-infographic looksmaxxing** — `@glowuptrickz` for Bloat Down. Visual: clip-art medical diagrams, before/after transformations, problem→solution two-panel cards. It reads like a textbook, not an ad. Product: App Store link in the bio only. Evidence: 1.6M followers, 47.4M likes.

**Bold-headline health infographic** — `@mognutrition`. Visual: teal/blue slide, red-and-white headline, one graphic per slide. Evidence: "Dismorphism (Pay close attention)" 422.4K views on 90 followers.

**Before/after face-photo infographic** — `@jacosahur`. Visual: all-caps title card, then before/after photos joined by an arrow. Evidence: "HOW TO DEBLOAT WITH SOFTMAXXES" 31.7K on 304 followers.

**Dark discipline / "sigma"** — Reset Club (the.atomic.reset). Visual: high-contrast black-and-white stills, grain, red accents, lines like "rebuild your life in the next 6 months". Evidence: top post 10.4M, 204.6K followers. It sells a Notion template, so distribution is strong and monetisation is weak.

**Rage-bait two-slide** — `@datingwithmads` (SGE). Structure: slide 1 a selfie with "Sometimes you just gotta traumatise these men back", slide 2 the chat screenshot. No product. Evidence: 5.8M views, 104.8K shares, 4.6× the account median. The cheapest of SGE's four nominated formats.

**Text receipt** — SGE's dating formats. Content is a screenshot of messages, shown as evidence, not advice. Reach: "Text receipt roast" 121M, "POV + text receipt slideshow" 35M, "Boyfriend checklist" 14.6M. Visuals not seen.

**Comparison slideshow on fandom debates** — WebComics. Mechanism: argument, not information. Evidence: 4M views on one post, app at about $100K revenue a month. Slides not seen.

**Theme-page value slideshow with a slide-4 CTA** — Lino Leighton for AbMaxx. Niche: abs, and "almost any consumer app niche".
- Structure: 5–6 slides. HOOK → VALUE → VALUE → VALUE → CTA → VALUE. The text says slide 4 for the CTA. The diagram shows slide 5. Test both by niche. "Every extra slide is another chance for someone to leave."
- Slides: one idea per slide, under 25 words, readable in 1–2 seconds. Drafted with a ChatGPT prompt, then rewritten by hand.
- Hook: one of four templates, see Hook shapes. "Simple hooks almost always beat clever ones."
- Product: slide 4 or 5. See Product Insertion for the CTA slide recipe.
- Visual: Pinterest images in one aesthetic. Seen: a sunset mountain photo, dark gym and outdoor photos with short text.
- Sound: chosen to match the aesthetic. Seen: the sound "hey".
- Metadata: #FYP #ForYouPage #Viral plus the niche keyword once or twice.
- Evidence: none given. His shown account had 37 followers and 0 likes.

**Notification slideshow** — Pedro Pérez, 10 Sep 2026. Niche: emotional story, any app with a push notification.
- Structure: 3 slides. Slide 1: the hook story over a recurring AI character (a woman crying in bed). Slide 2: a phone lock screen with a long iMessage from "Dad", and below it the app's own notification. Slide 3: a nostalgic payoff photo (a dad building furniture with a small daughter) with an emotional closing line.
- Hook: a first-person story that ends on a message. The blurred slide reads roughly "…my dad texted me this".
- Product: slide 2. The app notification is the CTA, "introduced naturally within the story rather than making the first slide feel like an advertisement."
- Sound: matched to the emotion. Both posts use "Love In The Dark".

## Hook shapes

The shapes below are the ones the evidence supports. Each has two or three verbatim examples with the result.

- **Numbered promise.** Count + category noun + a discomfort or promise word. "5 quiet signs your testosterone is COOKED" 437.2K (`@testo.cat`). "5 things that will pull you out of your dopamine addiction" 8.1M (Vent Now). "Every man needs 4 discipline habits" 739.8K (`@male_aestheticcc`). Lino's templates, no results given: "5 reasons why…", "5 things I wish I knew before…". Pedro's kill rule for these: below 3K views, kill; above 10–15K, replicate with small variations.
- **Keyword as hook.** The search term is the whole line. "Best struggle meals" (Nicholas's SEO example). "Healthy snack ideas" 7.6M (`@healthyyinspoo`). "PULL DAY" 1.7M and 1.6M (`@captaingreek_`).
- **Confession.** First person, a story, no claim. "he asked if I'd deleted the apps, we'd only been on two dates" 177K (`@mei.dates`). "I take a lot of pictures…" 19.5M over 3 posts (SGE). "lessons from 5 years of dating apps" 512K (`@mei.dates`).
- **Provocation.** A line that makes one side of the audience angry. "Sometimes you just gotta traumatise these men back" 5.8M (`@datingwithmads`). "what men don't understand about women (but REALLY should)" 7.9M (`@selfbysarah`). Nicholas's SEO version: an expensive ingredient on purpose.
- **Self-test.** The reader checks themself against a list. "signs of a slow burn vs. situationship / how to tell the difference" 2.2M (`@selfbysarah`). "Dismorphism (Pay close attention)" 422.4K (`@mognutrition`). "talking stage rules (for the girls with anxious attachment)" 24.9K (`@lindalewiston3`). Lino's template, no results given: "Stop doing this if you want…".
- **Receipt.** A screenshot of messages as evidence. Pedro's notification format puts the message on slide 2. The story hook ends on roughly "…my dad texted me this" (1.2M, and 2.0M for the replica). "Text receipt roast" 121M, "POV + text receipt slideshow" 35M (SGE dating formats). `@datingwithmads` slide 2 is the chat screenshot.
- **Overheard quote.** A line someone said, in quotation marks. "remember it's okay to skip the gym today" 2.7M (`@strongermobile`). "I've benched 100kg" 2M. "You've changed" 40.5K.
- **How-to guide with a series number.** "how to love someone who gets anxious in relationships" 7.5M (`@heather.xoxo`). "a guide on how to be a better girlfriend (part 5)" 2.6M. The "part 5" implies four you missed.
- **Parenthetical proof line.** A count + "rules/hacks/ways" title, then a parenthetical that proves it. "5 rules for our relationship i stole from my parents (they know something, we dont)" 50.4K (`@lin444782`). "4 rules we made after we almost broke up last year (these actually saved us)" ~45K. "feminine ways to make him feel seen (6 years in)" 32K.
- **Curiosity gap in a parenthetical.** "physical anxiety symptoms that sound fake (until you've had them)" (`@sydneysynced`). "5 rules for our relationship I stole from my parents (they know something, we don't)" 139.2K (`@lindalewiston3`).
- **Tension line.** SGE: "The strongest hooks introduce something intriguing, emotional or unexpected, without immediately giving everything away." "How did I find this—" (Locket). "Kind of chic..." 1.4M, 6.6K shares. "wait… let me grab my camera" (audio). Lino's template, no results given: "Here's exactly why…". Brain's draft cover uses the same shape: "Here's Exactly How To Unlock Your Dream Physique… (Step By Step)".
- **Product-slot listicle.** A list of apps has a native slot for yours. "4 apps that will make you disgustingly productive" (`@sydneysynced`). "Apps I use as a hybrid athlete" 672.7K and "Apps I use as a gym/run enthusiast" 174.7K, the top two posts on their grid (Laur's screenshot).
- **Save instruction in the hook.** "save this before your next first date — these questions beat two hours of small talk" 213.2K (`@mei.dates`).

Rules across shapes:
- Repeat the line, change the photo. `@lindalewiston3` posted "feminine ways to talk to him" three times: 61.2K, 39.8K, 24.1K.
- The line does not set the number. `@strongermobile` posted "remember it's okay to skip the gym today" at 2.7M and at 3801.
- Squeeze a hit before you hunt the next one. Lino's variation set: "5 reasons you still don't have abs" → "5 mistakes stopping you from getting abs" → "4 things I stopped doing to finally see my abs". Vary the hook, keep the topic, visual and CTA.
- Tie the line to a known person. Salim: "learning app? use nikola tesla or einstein. looksmaxxing app? use marlon or clavicular."
- Hookless posts also win. SGE: "many winning videos barely promoted the app, or had no obvious hook at all." HelloTalk hit 21.8M views without a traditional hook.

## Saves, not watch time

A slideshow has no watch time in the usual sense. The metrics are the swipe-through, the full-watch rate and the save.

- Laur's benchmark for a 7-second slideshow: "The % of watched full video = above 40% and an avg watch time of 6s would be nice".
- Flops come from the hook and the slide quality. Brain: "higher quality better looking slides keep the retention high and retention impacts the views massively".

- Vent Now: 99K bookmarks on 1.8M views, a save rate near 5.5%. cray cray: 85K saves on 2.8M views. The viewer saves the questions to use later.
- Give the viewer something to keep. Lists, tables, questions, workout splits.
- Burn the save prompt into the art. `@gymmadesimple` puts a "SAVE THIS FOR LATER" badge on every slide.
- Put the save instruction in the hook. `@mei.dates`: "save this before your next first date — these questions beat two hours of small talk" 213.2K.
- Measure the leak at every transition (Tjay): qualified view → CTA exposure → action → delivery → consumption → continuation → product outcome. Metrics: save rate, qualified profile-visit rate, click or install rate, unique delivery rate, resource consumption, voluntary continuation, qualified conversion. Give every campaign and creative a unique ID.
- Run a three-arm holdout: A direct action or profile, B requested resource, C resource plus optional conversation. Compare qualified conversions, time to conversion, complaints or blocks, human time, tool cost and retention. "The comment path may produce more visible engagement and fewer actual customers. That is a loss."
- "Reach is not revenue. Neither is a comment."
- A small post can beat a big one. Ethan Nguyen, from an AI UGC operator: "one piece of content got about 12k views and 202 bookmarks. Another got 50k views. The smaller post drove more signups." "Views are visible. Intent is valuable. Those are not the same thing."
- Salim on why TikTok pushes them: "i was saving them, sitting through every slide, engaging like crazy, and i didn't even notice i was doing it."

## Product Insertion & Conversion

- Default: keep the app out of the slides and in the bio. `@mei.dates`, `@testo.cat`, `@glowuptrickz` and the Vent Now personas all do this. Do not put a hard call to action in the content.
- Option 2: product on the late slides as the payoff. cray cray ends on in-app screenshots with the logo. Locket Widget ($300K MRR, 17.2M views) uses "two soft-sell calls to action on slides three and four".
- Option 2b, the most explicit CTA slide recipe: Lino's slide 4 or 5. First person, as a continuation of the value: "I've been using this app AbMaxx to help me finally lose lower belly fat." The slide also holds the App Store card with logo and name, plus two app screenshots. The purpose is logo recognition on the second exposure: "they will recognize the logo and more likely to buy the 2nd time." "Don't make slide 1 'DOWNLOAD MY APP!!!'".
- Option 2c: the CTA as a notification on slide 2. Pedro's notification format shows the app's own push notification under a text message on the lock screen. The story continues on slide 3. See Format Specs.
- Put a short app name on the slide, never a URL. Brain: "it's more likely for you to remember a short name rather than a full on website link, conversions for a whole link would be close to zero". Brain's own rule on timing: "the app gets introduced naturally near the end".
- Option 2d, confirmed on a real post, not on an X screenshot: the last-slide CTA. `@testo.cat`, pinned post, slide 7 of 7. The slide holds the headline "Lock in and BOOST your T naturally 👇". It holds an App Store card, "THRIVE: Male Health Habits — Boost Testosterone Naturally". It holds a phone screenshot of the app's habit list. Slides 2–6 are numbered value slides. It is Lino's recipe on the last slide instead of slide 4. Post: 90.1K views, 6,048 likes, 1,620 saves.
- Option 2e, confirmed on a real post: the text-only last-slide mention. `@lindalewiston3`, "6 dates you should go on before defining the relationship" (150.2K views), slide 6 of 6. Groundly is named once, inside the body of tip 6, as a first-person habit: "i do emotional check-in in the Groundly app and a grounding reset in the same spot, and doing both is what helps me come back grounded enough to trust what my gut is really saying." Plain white text over a dusk-sky photo. No card, no screenshot, no logo, no link, no download word. The softest CTA of the four accounts opened. The slot is not fixed. On the "5 rules for our relationship I stole from my parents" post (139.4K views), Groundly sits on slide 4 of 6, inside a tip on arguments: "that's when i use Groundly app to sort what i'm reacting to and get emotionally grounded, so the argument stays about the problem and not about him." The tip that best fits the app carries the sentence.
- Captions do not name the app. On the four accounts opened (`@testo.cat`, `@mei.dates`, `@lindalewiston3`, `@the.modern.man`), every caption is hashtags only or empty. The only caption pointer is a hashtag, `#groundingbeforedate` on `@lindalewiston3`.
- Three of four bios carry no app link. `@mei.dates` uses one bio word, "coffeemeetsbagel girlie", and nothing else. `@lindalewiston3` has no link. `@testo.cat` links a linktree. `@the.modern.man` links Instagram and promotes no app.
- Option 3: product on slide two for one beat, then the value. Only the "wait… let me grab my camera" format does this. Its CTA is a possession claim, "(Best purchase of my…", not an instruction.
- Shape a hook that holds the product. "4 apps that will make you disgustingly productive".
- Force comments at the topic, not at the app. Pedro: ask a question ("Which friend would you challenge to a bench press competition?"), ask for a word ("Comment 'I COMMIT' if you're actually going to lose weight before summer."), or use controversy in the content. Do not make the app the thing in doubt: "It's the RIGHT comments."
- The conversion signal is the comment section. Lino: comments full of "what's this app?", "app name?", "this app looks sick" mean the format converts. Double down on that combination.
- Expect lower intent. Lino: "Slideshow traffic is sometimes lower-intent than creator-led content or paid acquisition." Judge by economics: "Optimize for customers."

**Choose the CTA path from the job** (Tjay). Ask four questions. How much intent does the viewer have. How complex is the next decision. Can the value be delivered immediately. Does a conversation improve the outcome.
- The map: low intent + low complexity → save or follow. Medium + low → direct link or app-store path. Medium + medium → profile or resource. High + medium → resource with optional reply. High + high → human conversation.
- The six paths. *Save or follow*: educational post, early audience. *Direct product action*: the product is easy to understand and the store page matches the post. *Profile bridge*: no clean direct action, or the viewer needs more proof, and the profile continues the same argument. *Requested resource*: comment a word, receive an asset that must exist first. *Optional DM conversation*: only when the viewer's context changes the answer. *Human handoff*: custom or regulated work.
- "One primary CTA per post." Five jobs in one post make the analytics useless.
- Viewer effort must not exceed the expected value of the next step. "Every extra step is a leak."
- Our real posts on the map: `@testo.cat` is a direct product action (App Store card). `@mei.dates` and `@lindalewiston3` are profile bridges (bio word, text mention, no link). None of the opened accounts uses a comment-to-DM path.

- Make the viewer pull. Alberto, quoted by Ethan Nguyen: "The user should feel like they found your app. Not like your app found them." A slideshow about a real problem, "with an interesting screenshot on slide five", that makes someone comment "wait what app is this?" is pull. "Download now" is push.
- Judge on paying users, not views. GLP-1 campaign: "A post that gets fewer views but brings paying users can be worth a lot more to you."
- Treat claimed revenue as unverified. Thrive: "That app isn't making money tho lol."

## Finding Ideas & Vetting Formats

- Copy a format. Do not invent one. Salim: "you're going to steal one that's already printing." Pedro: "identify the FORMAT and adapt it to your product."
- Train a research feed. A burner account, 20-30 minutes of liking app content, until the feed is app content. Then generate keywords with an LLM and save every top slideshow into a swipe file.
- Filter an account before you copy it. Salim's four tests: "an active, repeatable format pattern" · "posts daily" · "at least 100k views in the last 30 days, spread across posts, not from a single viral fluke" · "bonus: run by a normie, posting for fun or for content rewards, with no product behind it". The normie with no product is "a distribution channel sitting there unclaimed".
- Use the TikTok search filters. Pedro: search niche terms ("fitness apps", "gym apps", "workout apps"), then set Sort by = Like count and Date posted = This month. Ignore what went viral a year ago. Look for formats that "work repeatedly, not just one viral video."
- Pedro's three tests for a format: viral repeatedly, works across niches, and a natural CTA slot inside the story.
- When you autopsy a post, read the comments with the most likes. Pedro's other checklist items: hook, first image, character, storyline, slide count, pacing, captions and CTA.
- Kill and scale thresholds. Pedro: "any format that gets more than 10–15k views is worth replicating with small variations. If a format stays below 3k views, I kill it immediately." "The winners become templates. The losers become data."
- Search the niche keywords on TikTok, not the feed. Lino's searches for an abs app: "easiest way to get abs", "how to get abs", "fat loss tips", "things killing your gains", "lower belly fat". The signal: "a slideshow with strong engagement, high views, and active comment section, and a recent post date." Then "Copy that format almost exactly: same hook, same slide structure, just with your own images and CTA."
- Ask Ethan Nguyen's five questions before you copy a flex. 1. "What result is actually proven?" Revenue, run rate, views and downloads are not interchangeable. 2. "What must have happened for that result to exist?" 3. "What part of that machine existed before this app?" Creators, accounts, audience, paid knowledge, data. 4. "Why is the author showing me this particular part?" 5. "What is the thing they could lose tomorrow and still win again?" Separate product age from distribution age. Mau Baron took "roughly 3 years and 10 attempts" before the app that scaled.
- Do not copy one viral post. "copy it and you're copying luck, not a format." Look for "a wall of them".
- Autopsy the top posts. "what's the hook on slide 1? how many slides? where does the value land? what makes someone actually save it?" Paste them into Claude Code post by post.
- Re-validate on two fresh accounts before you scale ("format maxxing"). Proofs: `@jacosahur` at 304 followers, `@mognutrition` at 90.
- Structure competitor research. Nicholas's Steal Sheet columns: Account name · Hook / catchphrase · Type of content · CTA · What worked / didn't work · Keywords used · Hashtags. "Study 20 to 30 posts per competitor minimum."
- Test 3 formats per account per day. Diagnose the reason, not the number. Many views and no comments means the engagement mechanism is wrong, not the format. Change one thing: "If you change everything, you learn nothing."
- Know the SEO ramp before you quit. Month 1: 1,000-3,000 views/day. Month 2: 3,000-7,000. Month 3+: 10,000+. "3 months minimum, no less."
- Clone-maxxing works. Bloat Down copied Depuff AI's format 1:1 and reached six figures a month.

## Working with Creators

- Cheap domestic production. Salim: "hire your 13-15 year old cousins (or anyone in that age range who lives on tiktok already) to pump out slideshows at scale."
- Borrow native accounts. Pay Discord users to post on accounts they have run for years. Pay: "$100 at 100k views · $350 at 1M views · capped around $500 if it hits 2M". Add bonuses to buy loyalty. Volume: "DM 100+ a day" for about 20 yeses. Ask every recruit for their friends.
- Brief with exact assets. Patiri: "give them a brief with exact hook text, shot structure, and CTA placement." The GLP-1 campaign shipped a vault of slideshow formats, a prompt bank, a Gemini tutorial and live calls.
- Expect a small group to carry the result. GLP-1 campaign: $21,418 spent, 23.1M views, $2K to $25K MRR in four months. 139 creators submitted content. 60 had at least one post approved. 10 creators produced 21M of the 23.1M views. The top creator posted 324 times.
- Fund in stages. They began with $4K and topped up to $21K over four months.

## 2026-09-12 — Ten scrolled cat-care slideshows, read slide by slide

Source: `SCROLLED-SLIDESHOW-BATCH-2026-09-12.md`. Rahul found nine posts and one profile while scrolling. All slides, the top 20 comments and the raw JSON are on disk under `media/scrolled-batch-2026-09-12/`. This is the first section in this file built from full slide sets, not from grid screenshots. Nine posts, 9.33M views, all in the cat-care niche. Cost about $0.03.

**Hook patterns seen.**
- **Confession that pays off as a joke.** "Things I bothered the vet with as a first time cat owner." One line on slide 1, then worry / "Diagnosis:" on every slide. 3.59M (`@pelinclh`, 9 slides) and 2.52M (`@tommy_purrs`, 6 slides), posted one day apart, same sentence word for word. Comments name a third version ("the beanie slideshow") that ended sad. This is one format copied across three accounts inside a few weeks, and viewers opened the third copy expecting the twist. Confirms point 3 of this file: one slot template, rotated.
- **Numbered promise.** "5 enrichment ideas that take under 5 minutes to make" 1.20M, "7 SIGNS YOUR CAT is in PAIN" 760K, "5 things I did to raise a calm & desensitized cat" 295K. Same shape as `@testo.cat` and Vent Now.

- **Fear plus deadline** (`@mias.diary7`). "Your kitten's whole personality is being decided RIGHT NOW 😳 / And most people waste these weeks 👉", "Thinking one kitten is enough? Think again 👀", "How to stop your cat waking you at 5am / It's not random - here's the fix 👉". A two-line hook: the claim, then a pointer to the swipe.
- The phrase "first time cat owner / first time cat mom" is on slide 1 of four of the nine posts. The target reader is named in the hook.

**Slide counts.** 6, 7, 7, 7, 9, 9, 9, 12, 13. Median 9. The two joke posts are the shortest (6 and 9). The two hauls are the longest. `@mias.diary7` runs 7 or 9. This fills the "slide count" gap in the "To find out" list above: 7–9 is the working range for a tip list, and one idea per slide sets the count.

**Text density.** Three tiers, and the save rate follows the tier.
- One or two lines per slide (joke posts, the Filipino haul): 0.7–1.9% saves/view, 19–25% likes/view on the jokes.

- The rule the batch supports: the more a slide can be used later, the more it is saved. Jokes are liked and shared. Instructions are saved.

**Sound.** Five of nine use a licensed track ("Manic Monday", "One More Time", "that's amore", "Boundless Worship", "Sogni ancora"). Four use an original sound, and both 2M+ joke posts do. The two posts with the same hook and different sounds got 3.6M and 2.5M. No evidence in this batch that the sound decides the result. The joke format pairs with a meme audio; the tip format pairs with a soft track. That matches Pedro's rule: match the emotion, do not chase the trend.

**Visual patterns.**
