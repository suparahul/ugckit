# Organic Growth Learnings — Videos (planning)

The compact guide behind `VIDEO-ANATOMY.md` and `video-patterns.json`. It says why the slots and rules are there and where each position came from. Quotes are verbatim; the rest is Simplified Technical English. It is small on purpose: no hook banks, no full corpus. Every position is labelled **observed** (posts were read), **claimed** (an article or creator says so), **user decision** (a founder's call) or **hypothesis** (planned, not yet measured).

---

## What the evidence says together

1. **A video is an attention beat and a payoff.** "A UGC video is two parts: the hook (first 3 seconds, most important) and the demo (showing the product)" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 1). The organic benchmarks agree on the shape and differ on the timing: Potto, Roamy and Stronger put a face first and the real app or its result at about 2–6 s (observed).
2. **The attention beat does not need a human face.** A subject doing the task holds attention; a cat with a real input and a real result can replace face → demo (hypothesis, founder plan). A face, a cat and a scene all fill the same `attention` beat.
3. **One idea, one mechanism, one doubt — when selling.** "Answer one doubt, with a number or a visible result. Not five doubts weakly" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 1). Teaching, first-person experience and selling are three intents; explanation and how-to need no invented doubt, transformation or credential.
4. **Explanation earns an action.** Observation → meaning → one thing to do (hypothesis, founder plan; observed in slideshow self-tests).
5. **Everyday is not a pitch.** UMax creatives often show no app UI, and only 428 of 1,691 sampled posts name UMax (observed). A routine can carry the app as a quiet log screen with no name said. Gym scenery with caption-only promotion was weak: four accounts, 200 posts, 465,304 views (observed, Stronger). A routine needs a meaningful task.
6. **The real thing or nothing.** "No fake statistics, fake reviews, fake notifications or invented product screens" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 10). The app on screen is a real recording; a real pet is real footage; a live demo is a real capture paired with its real result.
7. **Vary the idea, not the noun.** "Do not write 15 hooks that are the same sentence with one noun swapped" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 9). This is why the kit keeps twelve hook jobs with fill slots instead of a hook list: a new wording fills a slot; it is not a new job.
8. **Repetition beats one outlier.** One Potto post holds 83.4% of its attributed reach (observed). Compare medians with the sample size; an example's views are not a promised result.

## The hook

- The hook job is what the first beat does; the channel is how (said, shown, written). A visual-only first beat is valid for `in_progress`.
- "A hook is a spoken line, not a title. Usually 6 to 14 words" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 5). At the speech ceiling of 15 words per 4 s, a 15-word hook needs a full 4 s beat.
- "The picture is part of the hook… The first frame must stop the scroll alone: something in motion, not a held pose" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 5).
- The on-screen hook and the spoken lines are not the same words (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 10). When the hook is spoken, the captions already show it; do not add a hook overlay with the same words.
- Fill slots carry facts. Elapsed time, a result, a credential, a request and a quotation come from the plan or a source, never from the writer.
- Framing (count, negative, audience call-out, lived experience, professional basis) modifies a job. "Everyone asks how I…", a day counter, a warning and POV are framings or slot fills, not new jobs.

## The product and the app

- **Where the sources disagree, the handle decides** (user decision, 2026-10-03). SCRIPT-LEARNINGS' "never in the first 3 seconds, never the last beat, named once in the turn" is a paid-pitch position (claimed). Organic discovery benchmarks show the product early (observed). A handle rule such as "the app is the payoff" allows a closing product result.
- Product role, name location, app presence and timing are four slots. An app can be the result and never be named; it can be named once and never shown.
- "What the script says must agree with what the real screen shows" (claimed, SCRIPT-LEARNINGS § 10). A hero string is copied from the real screen; a number planned before the screen exists is proposed copy until the screen is indexed.
- The app's real words never become an overlay. The real screen carries them.

## Cast, world and media

- A generated character is pinned by version and must be `live` before production; planning never launches casting.
- A real pet is a fixed subject shown only in supplied footage. Planning states the exact subjects, count and size per beat; two cats are two pins.
- Supplied footage is output media, never a generation reference. It carries its source, permission and checksum.
- A synthetic narrator never speaks over a visible face, is never a clone, and claims no credential. A real expert is supplied and verified.

## Length and timing

- Bands: 10, 15, 20, 30 and 35–60 s. Education "why" videos run 35–60 s and join several talking segments at sentence ends.
- Generated segments are planned at 3 s or more and generated at 5–15 s. A 2-second opener needs supplied footage or a supported trim.
- Beats meet end to start and cover the whole length; simultaneous media are panels inside one beat.
- An overlay stays up at least its words ÷ 3 s.

## Planning, not production

- The plan stops at an approved, feasible plan: no pictures, voices, keyframes, prompts or segments. P1 owns the cut.
- A requested format stays requested when production cannot make it yet; the lock lists the capability gap.
- Approval is of one exact revision. Any change to words, actions, timing, cast, sources, layout or overlays is a new revision and a new approval.
